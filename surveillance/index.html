What the system should solve

Useful visibility, not just more cameras.

Important areas are difficult to see

Camera placement should begin with the entrances, approaches, or activity you need to understand—not a preset device count.

Recordings need to be dependable

The cameras, network, storage, and recording settings must work together when footage is needed.

Footage should be easy to review

A well-configured system makes it practical to find recordings without searching through hours of unclear video.

Access needs to be controlled

Remote viewing and user permissions should give the right people access without sharing one administrative account.

Planning the system

What we evaluate.

We discuss what you want to see, when recordings matter, who needs access, and how the system should be used. We then evaluate lighting, practical mounting positions, cabling routes, network needs, and storage.

  • Coverage goals, available light, and useful viewing angles
  • Camera locations and Ethernet cabling routes
  • Recording method and expected footage-retention needs
  • Remote viewing and separate user permissions
  • Supported activity alerts and detection features

Common questions

Planning a security-camera project.

Can I view cameras from my phone?

Supported systems can provide secure remote viewing. We configure access and show authorized users how to view live video and recordings.

How long can recordings be kept?

Retention depends on storage capacity, camera count, image quality, and recording settings. We plan those factors around the project’s needs.

Does the system need internet access?

Local recording can continue without internet on a properly designed system, but remote viewing and online alerts require a working connection.

How do continuous and activity-based recording differ?

Continuous recording captures video throughout the selected schedule. Activity-based recording saves footage when supported detection rules are triggered. The right approach depends on the space and retention goals.

Does Blyx take over or repair existing camera systems?

No. Blyx focuses on planning and installing complete new systems that can be tested and handed off as one defined project.
