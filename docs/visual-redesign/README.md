# Blyx website visual redesign brief

This is the source of truth for the redesign's intent and constraints. Detailed design decisions belong in the living visual design guide created during Milestone 02.

## Objective and audience

Present Blyx as a residential service company with the clarity, restraint, and precision of a strong product brand. A homeowner should quickly understand the services, recognize the quality of the work, feel confident considering Blyx, and know how to begin an inquiry.

Design for quality-conscious homeowners willing to invest in reliable, professionally integrated technology. Blyx should feel attainable but substantial: above price-led and do-it-yourself options without claiming an ultra-luxury experience. Commercial positioning is outside this redesign and may receive dedicated pages in a separate effort.

## Central promise

**Dependable technology, thoughtfully integrated into your home.**

This is the primary idea a visitor should retain. Every page, section, image, interaction, and proof point must reinforce it or provide credible evidence for it.

- **Dependable:** appropriate design, reliable systems, careful installation, testing, and a usable handoff.
- **Thoughtfully integrated:** clean installation, necessary compatibility, coherent planning, and simple daily use.
- **Into your home:** decisions begin with the household, property, routines, and actual need—not a product catalog.

Supporting messages are: reliable where needed; secure and under the homeowner's control; installed cleanly; simple to understand and use; and planned around the home.

Networking, security cameras, and access control with automated entry are equal service pillars. Give them comparable prominence on the homepage and in primary navigation.

## Experience and visual direction

The intended emotional outcome is **confidence**. The site itself must demonstrate the care, tidiness, and professionalism expected from work performed in the home.

The visual character is sleek, professional, clear, visually appealing, spacious, and exact. Use strong typography, restrained color, deliberate asymmetry, generous whitespace, and a clear hierarchy. Avoid anything bloated, complicated, overwhelming, or dense. Prefer fewer, stronger elements and visible breathing room.

[Meter](https://www.meter.com/) is a reference for whitespace, high-quality marketing imagery, simplified equipment and architectural compositions, and images that frame or reinforce copy. Do not reproduce Meter's brand, layouts, or interaction effects.

The result must remain native to a service business. Show installation quality, the relationship between equipment and the home, and the customer outcome—not a catalog of devices.

## Image direction

Copy communicates the promise and next action. Imagery reinforces one adjacent idea. Authentic project material establishes proof.

- Make an installed device, installation detail, or customer outcome the subject.
- Retain only enough architecture to establish context. A surveillance composition may show the camera, eave, and relevant corner geometry without showing the whole house.
- Use object placement, structural lines, and negative space to frame or lead toward copy.
- Keep the subject crisp while selected low-information edges blend into the page through directional gradients, transparency, tonal grading, or off-canvas cropping. Avoid uniform cloudy vignettes.
- Create separate desktop and mobile crops when necessary.
- Favor controlled natural light, realistic materials, clean surroundings, and restrained contrast.
- Use polished photography or simplified realistic renders for marketing compositions. Use authentic Blyx photography for workmanship proof and case studies.
- Never imply that generated, rendered, or staged imagery depicts completed Blyx work.

Images support the composition rather than becoming default full-bleed backgrounds. Wider environmental photographs belong mainly in project stories where the complete setting matters.

## Interface and content

- Typography carries the primary visual weight; color supports hierarchy without filling every section.
- Spacing provides separation; avoid dense card grids and unnecessary containers.
- Navigation, buttons, links, and forms must be precise, understated, accessible, and consistent.
- Form fields require clear labels, visible focus, useful validation, and strong contrast.
- Motion may clarify state or add subtle polish. Do not use heavy parallax, scroll-driven spectacle, or motion required to understand content.
- Use **we** as the Blyx company voice without implying a particular team size or unsupported capacity.
- Lead with homeowner needs and outcomes, supported by concrete service detail and plain language.
- Demonstrate planning, clean installation, testing, handoff, and respect for the property.
- Keep pricing consultative. Explain project fit and the inquiry process without publishing packages or starting prices.

Exact fonts, colors, type scales, spacing tokens, form aesthetics, image masks, and component states are Milestone 02 decisions.

## Service area

Blyx serves Louisville Metro and Bullitt, Oldham, Shelby, and Spencer counties in Kentucky. Treat this as a quiet but clear qualification, not a primary marketing message. Place a concise note beneath or beside contact forms and in the footer. Collect the project's city or ZIP code, but allow submissions from outside the area.

## Available proof

Blyx has photography and one testimonial from one completed residential project spanning networking, surveillance, entry automation, and access control. Present it as one integrated project; do not divide or repeat it to imply multiple customers or a larger portfolio.

Attribute the testimonial using the client's first name and last-name initial. Keep the residence private. Blyx may state that it is fully insured. Do not imply unverified certifications, manufacturer authorizations, credentials, service claims, or experience.

## Boundaries and working method

- Do not use `/prototype` files or visual language as a design reference or implementation source.
- Do not redesign the logo, imitate Meter, present Blyx as a manufacturer, or position it as a discount installer, do-it-yourself substitute, luxury concierge, or whole-home automation firm.
- Do not add content or visual complexity without a communication purpose.
- Complete milestones in numerical order. For each task, read only this brief, the active issue, and its relevant completed dependency artifacts. Do not load the entire backlog.
- Each milestone must leave compact, durable decisions for the next. Resolve conflicts with this brief explicitly before implementation.

## Completion standard

The public pages use one coherent visual system; imagery supports copy; residential services remain clear; real work is represented honestly; primary flows are accessible and responsive; image-led pages meet performance targets; and contact, analytics, metadata, and structured data continue to work.
