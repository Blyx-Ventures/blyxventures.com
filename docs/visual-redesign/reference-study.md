# Blyx annotated visual reference study

_Issue #23 · Visual-direction reference board · September 2026_

---

## 🧭 Direction

The references support one direction: **precise residential technology, shown with calm confidence**. Blyx should use copy as the primary carrier of meaning, then place one tightly framed image beside or behind it to reinforce the same idea. The result should feel spacious and exact without becoming sterile, product-led, cinematic, or luxury-coded.

This study is directional, not a component specification. Fonts, color values, spacing scales, masks, and interaction states belong in the Milestone 02 visual design guide.

## 🖼️ Focused reference board

### Meter — composition and image blending

[![Meter hardware arranged on a pale tonal field](https://www.meter.com/_next/image?url=%2Fassets%2Fstatic%2Farray-0.avif&w=3840&q=75)](https://www.meter.com/)

_Purpose: establish the core relationship among headline, negative space, cropped equipment, and a quiet tonal field._[^1]

**Borrow for Blyx**

- Give a short headline visual priority and keep supporting copy compact.
- Arrange one or several related subjects with deliberate empty areas reserved for copy.
- Let subjects cross the frame edge so imagery feels composed rather than placed in a card.
- Blend low-information edges into the page field while keeping equipment edges crisp.

**Do not copy**

- Do not reuse Meter's lavender palette, floating hardware array, type treatment, or interface details.
- Do not inherit its enterprise positioning or make Blyx look like a hardware manufacturer.
- Do not repeat a single product-composition effect across every section.

### eero — direct homeowner language

[![eero campaign image for residential Wi-Fi](https://contentful.prod.eero.com/contentful/o8zdilputr65/4QfRr9jFc3Wa3AYyQTSjTp/8fa4647d89c0d8998714f26fb102cd13/Home-Carousel-1-1920-compressed.webp)](https://www.eero.com/)

_Purpose: show how technically complex networking can be introduced through concise, familiar homeowner language._[^2]

**Borrow for Blyx**

- Lead with the household problem or outcome before technical detail.
- Use plain, compact headlines that remain legible over or beside imagery.
- Keep the primary action obvious and subordinate product explanation to the main promise.

**Do not copy**

- Do not use eero's playful campaign voice, consumer-retail rhythm, carousel, or shopping patterns.
- Do not center individual products; Blyx sells planning, installation, and dependable operation.
- Do not let motion or changing slides carry information required for comprehension.

### SPAN — visible professional work

[![Close view of a SPAN electrical panel installation](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/628f26de26f0252b4094378b%2F68a3af890cc5729f27b7127f_SPAN%20hero%20Loop%20%28720p%2940%20full-poster-00001.jpg)](https://www.span.io/)

_Purpose: connect sophisticated home infrastructure to the people, tools, surfaces, and care involved in installation._[^3]

**Borrow for Blyx**

- Show equipment in a credible installation context rather than as an isolated catalog object.
- Crop closely enough that clean workmanship and material detail are readable.
- Use hands, tools, cable paths, mounting points, and finished surfaces as evidence of care.
- Pair process imagery with outcome-led copy instead of narrating every installation step.

**Do not copy**

- Do not use full-screen background video, oversized overlaid type, or scroll spectacle.
- Do not imitate SPAN's green accent, industrial product styling, or manufacturer claims.
- Do not stage generated installation imagery as completed Blyx work.

### Lutron — residential architecture as context

[![Modern residential interior with integrated lighting controls](https://www.lutron.com/us/sites/hub_americas/files/styles/content_two_thirds_desktop_narrow_2x/public/2025-04/seattle-rania-modal.webp?h=a2abd498&itok=QzJiLmVq)](https://www.lutron.com/us/en/lighting/residential-lighting)

_Purpose: show how architectural lines and controlled natural light can locate technology inside a real home._[^4]

**Borrow for Blyx**

- Use eaves, walls, ceilings, door frames, millwork, and sight lines to frame the relevant device or outcome.
- Favor clean, realistic materials, restrained contrast, and believable residential light.
- Crop to the smallest architectural area that explains placement and integration.

**Do not copy**

- Do not rely on aspirational whole-room photography when the technology or workmanship is unreadable.
- Do not adopt luxury-residence signaling, lifestyle imagery, or a whole-home automation position.
- Do not make architecture the subject; the installed result and homeowner benefit remain the subject.

## 📐 Shared visual grammar

| Dimension | Blyx rule | Evidence in the board | Rejection test |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Copy hierarchy | One clear promise, one short explanation, one primary action | Meter, eero | If the image disappears, the section still communicates its purpose and next step |
| Whitespace | Preserve a calm field around the headline and action | Meter | No decorative object may occupy space needed for reading or hierarchy |
| Subject crop | Show the device, relevant construction edge, and only enough context to understand placement | Meter, SPAN | Reject crops that show an entire property when one corner, eave, doorway, or rack proves the point |
| Architectural framing | Use structural lines to contain the subject or lead toward copy | Lutron | Reject rooms that are visually impressive but do not clarify the service or result |
| Image blending | Fade or grade only low-information edges; keep the proof area crisp | Meter | Reject uniform haze, cloudy vignettes, and gradients that obscure workmanship |
| Human presence | Show purposeful work or lived benefit without generic lifestyle theater | SPAN | Reject stock-like posing and any scene that implies unsupported scale or capability |
| Proof | Use authentic Blyx photography for completed work and clearly distinguish polished marketing imagery | SPAN, Lutron | Never present generated, rendered, or staged imagery as a Blyx project |

## ✅ Direction for the visual studies

Every visual-direction study should demonstrate the same five conditions:

1. Copy remains understandable and actionable without the image.
2. One image reinforces one adjacent idea.
3. The subject is crisp, tightly cropped, and placed with intentional negative space.
4. Residential architecture supplies context without becoming luxury scenery.
5. Motion, if present, adds polish but carries no essential meaning.

Evaluate each study by asking: _Does this make Blyx feel like a dependable residential integrator whose work is planned carefully, installed cleanly, and easy to live with?_ If the answer depends on a visual effect, a product glamour shot, or an expansive property image, the study is outside the direction.
