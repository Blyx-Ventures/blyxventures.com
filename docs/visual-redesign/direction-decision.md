# Blyx visual direction decision

_Issue #24 · Approved composition direction · September 2026_

---

## ✅ Decision

Approve **Study A — Quiet precision** as the visual direction for the Blyx redesign.

The direction gives copy the largest, calmest area of the composition and uses a tightly cropped installation image as adjacent proof. The image may meet or cross the viewport edge, but it does not sit behind essential copy. A directional blend joins low-information image edges to the page field while the installed equipment remains crisp.

## 📊 Evaluation

| Creative-brief criterion | A — Quiet precision | B — Framed threshold | C — Immersive field |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Copy leads | Strong: headline owns the largest uninterrupted field | Strong: copy remains separate, but the framed image has similar weight | Weak: image establishes the experience before the copy |
| Imagery supports one idea | Strong: organized equipment substantiates dependable installation | Strong: reader placement substantiates integrated access | Mixed: attic work provides proof but also becomes the atmosphere |
| Residential, not commercial | Strong: authentic residential-project proof and local service context | Strong: recognizable residential materials and entry detail | Strong: authentic residential construction context |
| Spacious and exact | Strong: few elements, controlled overlap, generous negative space | Strong: deliberate grid and architectural crop | Mixed: visually clear but the full field leaves less calm reading space |
| Service-native, not product-led | Strong: the label identifies completed work rather than a device | Strong: technology is visibly integrated into the building | Strong: process is visible, though the image can overwhelm the outcome |
| Avoids image dominance | Strong | Acceptable | Fails as the default pattern |
| Avoids heavy visual effects | Strong: limited edge blend only | Strong: simple crop and rule | Weak: depends on a dark overlay for legibility |

## 🧱 Approved composition principles

- Reserve roughly half the desktop hero for an uninterrupted copy field.
- Use one image to reinforce the adjacent promise; do not require the image to explain the offer or action.
- Crop toward the installed device, cable path, mounting detail, or usable outcome.
- Preserve architecture only where it clarifies placement or residential context.
- Blend only low-information edges into the page field; keep proof details sharp.
- Stack copy before imagery on narrow screens so the reading order remains intact.
- Label authentic project photography as completed residential work when context would otherwise be ambiguous.

Study B's framed architectural crop is approved as a secondary section pattern when the surrounding construction materially explains the service. It is not the default hero because repeated tall image frames would make the site feel editorial and template-driven.

## 🚫 Rejected traits

- Full-bleed photography as the default hero treatment
- Essential copy placed over detailed or high-contrast imagery
- Dark overlays used primarily to repair text legibility
- Lifestyle or property imagery whose setting is more prominent than the installed result
- Repeated portrait frames that make every service conform to one editorial layout
- Floating product arrays, catalog presentation, or manufacturer-like compositions
- Motion, parallax, or image transitions required to understand the page
- Decorative gradients, haze, or vignettes across the proof area

## 🎯 Downstream test

A future layout conforms to the approved direction when its message, service scope, and primary action remain clear with the image removed—and when adding the image contributes one specific piece of context or proof without changing that meaning.
